Eagles Name Applebee's General Manager Manning Barkby as New Head Coach

Covington, Georgia native ends a decade in casual dining personnel management to take over the team he grew up worshipping. Bananas have been banned from the practice facility, effective immediately.

By Frank Schlossberg Senior NFL Correspondent Updated 14 minutes ago · 6 min read

PHILADELPHIA. In a move that blindsided the league office, the Eagles' front office, and reportedly Coach Barkby's own employer of more than a decade, the Philadelphia Eagles announced Tuesday that Manning Barkby, 45, of Covington, Georgia, has been hired as the franchise's new head coach.

Barkby, who spent the last ten-plus years as General Manager of an Applebee's Neighborhood Grill & Bar location in the greater Covington metro area, signed a multi-year deal with the club shortly after 3:00 p.m. Eastern. He delivered his first address to the team less than ninety minutes later, in what front-office sources are calling "a confidence-building moment, broadly speaking."

League observers described the hire as unconventional. Coach Barkby holds no prior coaching experience at the professional, collegiate, or high-school level. He has, however, managed a kitchen, a bar, a hostess station, and an outdoor patio simultaneously for the better part of a decade, which a source inside the Eagles' front office characterized as "frankly the same thing."

From Riblets to Run Game

Barkby's path to Philadelphia represents what one anonymous AFC scout described as "the casual-dining-to-coaching pipeline that the league has been quietly piloting for some time." Born in Pennsylvania and a lifelong Eagles fan, Barkby relocated to Georgia in 2015 for what family members confirmed was originally intended to be a short stay.

During his tenure at the Covington Applebee's, Barkby personally oversaw the operational rollout of the 2-for-$25 promotion, navigated three separate freezer outages, and built what employees described as "a culture of accountability and bottomless boneless wings." He is widely credited with stabilizing the location's weekend brunch traffic.

"Coach Barkby is somebody who knows how to put bodies in seats," said Eagles General Manager Howie Roseman in a brief statement Tuesday evening. "That is, ultimately, what we are trying to do here as well."

The Pajama Question

Tuesday's introductory address has already generated significant discussion online, owing primarily to Coach Barkby's choice of attire. Reporters in attendance confirmed that Barkby delivered the speech in a full-body Jack Skellington patterned onesie, with the hood up, while standing on a wooden locker-room bench and holding what appeared to be a slice of pizza.

Sources close to the new coach described the outfit as deliberate. "Manning is a sleep-first guy," one family member told Philly Sports Daily. "If he wakes up in it, he coaches in it. He has been very consistent on that for about eleven years."

Eagles ownership, when asked whether the team would impose a dress code on its new head coach, declined to comment, except to note that "the contract does not specify business casual, no."

Banana Ban Effective Immediately

The most concrete policy change announced Tuesday concerns the team's nutrition program. In his first official act, Coach Barkby has banned all bananas, banana-based products, and any depictions, photographs, illustrations, plush toys, or verbal references to bananas from the team's practice facility, training table, locker room, charter aircraft, and family seating section at Lincoln Financial Field.

Coach Barkby cited "long-standing personal reasons" for the policy. A 2009 incident at a small apartment in Pennsylvania, in which Barkby reportedly defended himself against a relative wielding a banana by means of a kitchen fork, has been described by family as the inciting event. "Manning has come a long way," his brother said in a phone interview. "But he's not there yet, and the team needs to respect that."

The league office, asked about enforcement, indicated only that "every coaching staff is permitted to set its own dietary policy, within reason. We will review the 'within reason' part separately."

Player Reactions Mixed

Player reactions inside the locker room ranged from receptive to deeply confused. Linebacker reactions were captured in the figure above, with at least one veteran defender (number 34) seen with his head bowed during the address. A team spokesperson clarified that the gesture was "a personal moment of focus, not, as has been speculated online, the early stages of a resignation."

One offensive lineman, granted anonymity to speak freely, offered an evaluation of the new coach. "He brought pizza," the player said. "I don't know him, but he brought pizza. That counts for something."

A defensive back, asked whether the team was aware of Coach Barkby's prior coaching experience, paused for several seconds before responding. "I assumed there was some," he said. "There has to be some, right? Somewhere?"

A Family Affair

Coach Barkby's family is expected to relocate from Covington in the coming weeks. His son, Rushane, known throughout the household as "Worm," is reportedly excited about the move and is expected to attend the team's home opener. Asked whether the nickname would follow him to Philadelphia, Coach Barkby was emphatic. "Worm is Worm," he said. "Worm doesn't change because of zip code."

The new head coach's first regular press conference is scheduled for Wednesday afternoon. Multiple outlets have requested clarification on whether the press conference will also be conducted in sleepwear. The team has not yet responded.

First Press Conference Excerpt

Late Tuesday, the Eagles released a brief excerpt from Coach Barkby's first informal media availability. The full transcript will be published Wednesday. Notable lines below.

On his offensive philosophy: "Four quarters. Four toppings. That's the way I've always thought about it. Some games you go classic, some games you go combination. You read the room."

On his expectations for the season: "Look, I managed an Applebee's through the 2017 Mother's Day brunch rush. The Super Bowl is a Tuesday by comparison."

On the pizza he was visibly holding: "This is from a place down the street. It's not relevant to the announcement. I just got hungry."
